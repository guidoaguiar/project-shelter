# Finding Water
tags: water, source, dew, rain, spring, dehydration

Where to look for water when taps are dry or you are away from supply, ranked by effort and safety.

## Where to look

1. **Rain.** The safest wild source. Catch it with a tarp angled into a container.
2. **Springs and seeps.** Look at the base of cliffs and where rock layers meet.
3. **Flowing streams.** Prefer fast, clear water upstream of settlements and livestock.
4. **Lakes and ponds.** Take from clear water away from the edge and stagnant shallows.
5. **Dew.** Wipe grass with a cloth at dawn and wring it out. Slow but real.
6. **Dry stream beds.** Dig at the outside of a bend, at the lowest point.

## Reading the land

- Water runs downhill: follow valleys and drainage lines.
- Green vegetation lines, willows, reeds and cottonwoods mark shallow groundwater.
- Converging animal trails, and birds flying low at dawn and dusk, often lead to water.
- Insects, especially bees and mosquitoes, are rarely far from it.

## Do not drink

Seawater, urine, alcohol or water from vehicle radiators. All of them speed dehydration or poison you.

> **WARNING** Treat every wild source before drinking, however clear it looks. See *Making Water Safe*.

## Ration sweat, not water

Drink what you have when you need it. Reduce loss instead: rest in shade, work in the cool hours, and keep covered.

# Making Water Safe
tags: water, purification, boiling, bleach, chlorine, filter, sodis, iodine

Clear water can still carry bacteria, viruses and parasites. Treat everything you did not store yourself.

## Step 0: clarify

Let cloudy water settle, then pour it through a cloth or coffee filter. Disinfection works far better on clear water.

## Boiling (most reliable)

Bring to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**. Above 2000 m (6500 ft), boil for **3 minutes**. Let it cool covered. Boiling kills all pathogens but does not remove chemicals.

## Household bleach

Use plain, unscented bleach with no additives.

| Bleach strength | Per litre | Per gallon |
|---|---|---|
| 6% sodium hypochlorite | 2 drops | 8 drops |
| 8.25% sodium hypochlorite | 2 drops | 6 drops |

Double the dose if the water is cloudy, coloured or very cold. Stir and wait **30 minutes**. It should smell faintly of chlorine. If not, repeat the dose and wait 15 minutes more. Bleach loses strength with age: replace opened bottles yearly.

## Filters

Pump, squeeze and gravity filters rated 0.2 micron or finer remove bacteria and parasites. Most do **not** remove viruses. Where human sewage is a risk, filter first and then disinfect.

## Sunlight (SODIS)

Fill clear PET bottles of 2 L or less with clear water. Lay them in full sun for **6 hours**, or 2 days if overcast. Does not work on cloudy water.

## Iodine

Tincture of iodine 2%: 5 drops per litre of clear water, 10 if cloudy, wait 30 minutes. Not for pregnant women, people with thyroid conditions, or use beyond a few weeks.

> **WARNING** No field method removes fuel, pesticides or heavy metals. Avoid water with an oily sheen, a chemical smell or dead vegetation around it.

# Storing and Collecting Water
tags: water, storage, rainwater, catchment, solar still, containers

How much water to keep, how to keep it, and how to gather more from rain and plants.

## How much

Plan on **4 L (1 gallon) per person per day** for drinking and basic cooking and hygiene. Two weeks for a household of four is about 225 L. Heat, illness, nursing and hard labour raise the need.

## Storage

- Use food-grade containers. Avoid anything that held milk, juice or chemicals.
- Wash, then sanitise with 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per litre of water; swirl and rinse.
- Treated tap water needs nothing added. Store cool and dark, off bare concrete.
- Label with the date and rotate every 6 to 12 months.

## Rainwater catchment

**1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof yields 1 litre.** A 100 m² roof in a 10 mm shower gives about 1000 L.

- Divert the first few minutes of flow (the first flush), which carries dust and droppings.
- Screen inlets against mosquitoes and debris. Keep tanks covered and dark.
- Treat before drinking.

## Transpiration bag

Tie a clear plastic bag over a leafy branch of a non-toxic tree in full sun. Water collects in the low corner over the day. Typical yield is a cup or so per bag, so use several.

## Solar still

A pit covered with plastic sheet, a stone in the centre and a cup below. It works, but the yield is often less than the sweat lost digging it. Use it only where the ground is damp and you have nothing better.
