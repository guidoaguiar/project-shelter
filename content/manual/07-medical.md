# Stopping Severe Bleeding
tags: medical, first aid, bleeding, tourniquet, pressure, wound packing, trauma

A person can bleed to death in minutes. Controlling bleeding comes before everything except your own safety.

## Steps

1. **Expose** the wound. Find where the blood is actually coming from.
2. **Press.** Put cloth or gauze on it and push hard with both hands. Do not lift to check for at least 10 minutes.
3. **Pack** deep wounds in the groin, armpit or neck base: feed gauze or clean cloth into the wound until full, then press on top.
4. **Bandage** tightly over the dressing once bleeding slows. If blood soaks through, add more on top; do not remove the first layer.

## Tourniquet (life-threatening limb bleeding)

1. Place it **5 to 8 cm above the wound**, never over a joint. If you cannot see the wound, go high on the limb.
2. Tighten until the bleeding **stops**. It will hurt. That is expected.
3. Secure it and write the time on it or on the casualty.
4. Do not loosen or remove it. That is for medical staff.

Improvise with a strip of cloth at least 4 cm wide and a stick as a windlass. Cord, wire and belts work poorly.

## Then

Lay the casualty down, keep them warm, and get help. Pale clammy skin, a fast weak pulse and confusion are signs of shock.

> **WARNING** This entry is first aid, not treatment. Severe bleeding always needs professional care as fast as it can be reached.

# Wound Care and Infection
tags: medical, first aid, wound, infection, cleaning, dressing, burns

Away from hospitals, small wounds become dangerous through infection. Cleaning is the treatment.

## Cleaning

1. Stop the bleeding with pressure.
2. Wash your hands or wear gloves.
3. **Irrigate** with a large volume of drinkable water under pressure: at least half a litre, more for dirty wounds. A plastic bag with a pinhole or a bottle with a pierced cap makes a jet.
4. Pick out any visible dirt with clean tweezers.
5. Do not pour alcohol or peroxide into the wound. They damage tissue.

## Closing

Small clean cuts can be drawn together with tape strips. **Leave open** any wound that is dirty, a puncture, an animal or human bite, or more than a few hours old. Sealing dirt inside causes abscesses. Pack lightly with damp gauze and cover.

## Dressing

Cover with a clean dressing. Change it daily and whenever wet or dirty. Check the wound every time.

## Signs of infection

- Redness that spreads, warmth, increasing swelling and pain
- Pus or foul smell
- Red streaks running up the limb
- Fever or chills

Spreading redness, streaks or fever mean the infection is moving. Evacuate to medical care.

## Burns

1. Cool with cool running water for **20 minutes**. Not ice.
2. Remove rings and tight items before swelling starts.
3. Cover loosely with a clean non-stick dressing or cling film.
4. Do not burst blisters. Do not apply butter, oil or toothpaste.

Burns larger than the casualty's palm, or on the face, hands, groin or joints, need professional care.

# Fractures and Sprains
tags: medical, first aid, fracture, broken bone, splint, sprain, sling

Splinting stops movement, which reduces pain and prevents further damage on the way to care.

## Recognise

Pain, swelling, deformity, bruising, a grinding feeling, or refusal to use the limb. If unsure, treat it as broken.

## Splint

1. Check **circulation, sensation and movement** beyond the injury: is it pink and warm, can they feel touch, can they wiggle fingers or toes?
2. Pad the splint well, especially over bony points.
3. Immobilise the **joint above and the joint below** the break.
4. Tie firmly but not tightly, above and below the injury, never directly over it.
5. Recheck circulation, sensation and movement. If worse, loosen.

Materials: sticks, a rolled sleeping pad, folded cardboard, a trekking pole, or the casualty's other leg. A triangular cloth or the bottom of a shirt pinned up makes an arm sling.

## Open fractures

Bone showing or a wound over the break. Rinse, cover with a moist clean dressing, splint as it lies. Do not push bone back in. This is an urgent evacuation.

## Sprains

Rest, cool, compress with an elastic bandage and elevate. If the casualty cannot take four steps on it, treat as a fracture.

> **WARNING** Suspect spinal injury after any fall from height or high-speed impact. Do not move the casualty unless they are in immediate danger.

# Cold and Heat Injuries
tags: medical, hypothermia, heat stroke, heat exhaustion, frostbite, temperature

Body temperature has a narrow safe range. Both ends kill, and both are easier to prevent than to treat.

## Hypothermia

Watch for the **umbles**: stumbles, mumbles, fumbles and grumbles. Shivering that stops without rewarming is a late and serious sign.

1. Get out of wind and wet.
2. Replace wet clothing with dry.
3. Insulate from the ground and wrap fully: sleeping bag, blankets, then a tarp or foil layer outside.
4. Give warm sweet drinks and food if fully alert.
5. Put warm bottles at the armpits, chest and groin, wrapped in cloth.
6. Handle a severely cold casualty gently and keep them lying flat.

Do not rub limbs and do not give alcohol.

## Frostbite

Skin turns pale, hard and numb. Rewarm in warm (not hot) water, 37 to 39 °C, only if there is no chance of refreezing. Do not rub. Do not walk on thawed feet.

## Heat exhaustion

Heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, cramps. Rest in shade, loosen clothing, cool with wet cloths and sip water with a little salt.

## Heat stroke

**Confusion, collapse or seizures in a hot person is heat stroke** and can kill within the hour. Skin may be dry or wet.

1. Cool immediately by any means: immersion in cool water is best.
2. Otherwise soak the clothing and fan hard, with ice or cold packs at the neck, armpits and groin.
3. Evacuate urgently while continuing to cool.

# Dehydration and Rehydration
tags: medical, dehydration, diarrhoea, oral rehydration, ors, electrolytes

Diarrhoea and heat kill by draining water and salt. A simple mixed drink replaces both and has saved millions of lives.

## Signs

- Thirst, dark urine, passing little urine
- Headache, dizziness on standing, dry mouth
- Severe: sunken eyes, no tears, skin that stays tented when pinched, confusion

Pale straw-coloured urine is the target.

## Oral rehydration solution

To **1 litre of clean water** add:

- **6 level teaspoons of sugar**
- **half a level teaspoon of salt**

Stir until dissolved. It should taste no saltier than tears. Make it fresh every day.

## Giving it

- Sip steadily, a little and often. If vomiting, wait ten minutes and restart with spoonfuls.
- Adults: a cup or more after each loose stool. Children: about half that.
- Keep feeding. Continue breastfeeding infants.

## Get help when

There is blood in the stool, high fever, no urine for 8 hours, the casualty cannot keep fluids down, or they become drowsy or confused.

> **NOTE** Measure carefully. Too much salt is harmful, especially for children.
