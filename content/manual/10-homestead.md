# The Survival Garden
tags: garden, crops, calories, three sisters, potatoes, beans, growing

Salad does not keep anyone alive. A garden meant to feed people is planned around calories and storage.

## Calorie crops

| Crop | Why |
|---|---|
| Potatoes | Highest calories for the space, stores for months |
| Dry beans | Protein, stores for years, feeds the soil |
| Maize (corn) | Grain that stores dry and grinds to meal |
| Winter squash | Stores for months without processing |
| Cabbage, carrots, beets | Hardy, store well, ferment well |
| Onions and garlic | Flavour, store dry |

## The three sisters

An old companion planting: **maize, climbing beans and squash** together.

1. Sow maize in mounds about a metre apart.
2. When it is hand high, sow beans around each stalk. They climb the maize and add nitrogen.
3. Sow squash between the mounds. Its leaves shade out weeds and hold moisture.

## Practice

- Start small and expand. A neglected large plot yields less than a tended small one.
- Rotate crop families each year to limit disease and soil exhaustion.
- Mulch thickly with straw or leaves to save water and weeding.
- Sow a little every two weeks for a steady harvest rather than a glut.
- Grow what you already eat, and keep records in the Log.

# Seed Saving
tags: seeds, heirloom, garden, storage, open pollinated

Seed bought once can feed you indefinitely if you save it. This was ordinary knowledge three generations ago.

## Choose the right plants

Save seed from **open-pollinated or heirloom** varieties. Seed saved from F1 hybrids does not grow true to the parent.

Easiest to start with are the self-pollinators: **beans, peas, tomatoes, lettuce and peppers.** Squash, maize and brassicas cross readily with neighbours and need isolation.

## Harvest

- **Beans and peas:** leave pods on the plant until dry and rattling, then shell.
- **Tomatoes:** squeeze the seeds and gel into a jar with a little water. Leave 2 to 3 days until a film forms, which removes the germination-blocking coat. Rinse and dry on a plate.
- **Lettuce:** let it bolt and flower. Shake the dry seed heads into a bag.
- **Peppers:** take seed from fully ripe fruit and dry.
- **Squash:** scoop from fully mature fruit, wash and dry.

Always save from your healthiest, most productive plants, never from the leftovers.

## Dry and store

Dry until a seed snaps rather than bends. Store in paper envelopes inside an airtight jar, **cool, dark and dry**. Label with variety and year.

## How long seed lasts

| Crop | Years |
|---|---|
| Onion, parsnip | 1 to 2 |
| Maize, pepper | 2 to 3 |
| Beans, peas, carrot | 3 to 4 |
| Tomato, squash, brassicas | 4 to 6 |
| Cucumber, lettuce | 5 or more |

Test old seed by sprouting ten in a damp cloth. The number that sprout, times ten, is the percentage alive.

# The Root Cellar
tags: storage, root cellar, vegetables, potatoes, winter, preservation

A cool, dark, damp space keeps a harvest through winter with no power. Earth holds a steady temperature a metre or two down.

## Two climates

| Conditions | Crops |
|---|---|
| Cold and damp: 0 to 4 °C, 85 to 95% humidity | Potatoes, carrots, beets, parsnips, cabbage, apples |
| Cool and dry: 10 to 15 °C, 60 to 70% humidity | Winter squash, pumpkins, onions, garlic, dry beans |

## Rules

- Store only sound, unbruised produce. One rotting item spoils its neighbours.
- **Cure first:** potatoes for two weeks in the dark at mild temperature; squash and onions in warm dry air until skins harden.
- Do not wash roots. Brush off soil.
- Keep potatoes in complete darkness. Light turns them green and bitter; cut green parts away.
- Keep **apples away from potatoes** and carrots: the ethylene they give off causes sprouting and bitterness.
- Pack carrots and beets in boxes of damp sand or sawdust.
- Ventilate: one low inlet, one high outlet.
- Inspect weekly and remove anything turning.

## No cellar

- Bury a barrel or old chest freezer on its back, lid at ground level, covered with straw bales.
- A clamp: pile roots on straw, cover with more straw, then 15 cm of soil, leaving a straw-plugged vent at the top.
- An unheated room, crawl space or north-facing shed often does the job.

# Composting
tags: compost, soil, garden, fertility, waste

Compost turns kitchen and garden waste into the fertility that the next crop needs, with no bought fertiliser.

## The mix

Balance **browns** (carbon: dry leaves, straw, cardboard, wood chips) with **greens** (nitrogen: kitchen scraps, fresh grass, manure) at roughly **two or three parts brown to one part green** by volume.

## Build

1. Choose a spot on bare soil, at least a metre square. Smaller piles do not heat.
2. Start with coarse twigs for drainage.
3. Layer browns and greens, wetting each layer.
4. Keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge.
5. Turn it every week or two to add air.

A well-built pile heats to 55 to 65 °C within days, which kills weed seeds and most pathogens. It is finished when dark, crumbly and earthy smelling, in two to six months.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Smells rotten | Too wet or too green | Add browns, turn |
| Not heating | Too dry, too small or too brown | Water, add greens, build bigger |
| Attracts pests | Exposed food | Bury scraps in the centre |

## Leave out

Meat, fish, dairy, fats, diseased plants, and dog or cat waste.
