# Power Outage
tags: emergency, disaster, household, power outage, blackout, generator, carbon monoxide, refrigerator, food safety

Most outages last hours, some last days. The dangers are carbon monoxide from generators and heaters, spoiled food, and medical devices that stop working.

## Before

- Keep flashlights and spare batteries where you can find them in the dark. Avoid candles: they start fires.
- Know which medicines need cold storage and which devices need power. Ask your doctor or supplier about a backup plan.
- Put a battery-backed carbon monoxide alarm on every sleeping floor.
- Keep a full tank of fuel in the car and some cash at home. Card machines and pumps stop too.

## During

1. Keep the fridge and freezer **closed**. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours; a full freezer for about 48 hours, a half-full one for about 24.
2. Unplug appliances and electronics, so the surge when power returns does not damage them. Leave one lamp on to show when it is back.
3. Run generators **only outdoors**, far from windows, doors and vents. Never in a garage, basement or porch, even with the door open.
4. Never heat the home with a gas oven, stove, grill or camp stove.
5. In heat or cold, go to a cooling or warming centre rather than staying in an unsafe home.

## After

Throw out perishable food (meat, fish, eggs, dairy, leftovers) that has been above 4 °C (40 °F) for 2 hours or more. **When in doubt, throw it out.** See also Home Blackout Protocol under PRIORITIES.

> **WARNING** Headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea or confusion in several people at once can be carbon monoxide poisoning. Get everyone into fresh air and call for help.

Sources: Ready.gov, Power Outages (public domain); FoodSafety.gov / USDA, Food Safety in a Power Outage (public domain).

# Flood
tags: emergency, disaster, household, flood, flash flood, water, evacuation, turn around don't drown

Flooding is the most common natural disaster, and most flood deaths happen in vehicles. Moving water is far stronger than it looks.

## Before

- Learn whether your home is in a flood zone and what your evacuation route is.
- Move valuables and documents upstairs or into waterproof containers.
- Know how to turn off electricity and gas, and do it if told to.

## During

1. If told to evacuate, **go**. Move to higher ground.
2. **Turn around, don't drown.** About 15 cm (6 in) of moving water can knock a person down; about 30 cm (1 ft) can sweep a car away.
3. Do not walk, swim or drive through flood water. Stay off bridges over fast-moving water.
4. If trapped in a building, go to the highest level. Do not climb into a closed attic, where rising water can trap you. Go onto the roof only if you must, and signal for help.

## After

- Return only when officials say it is safe.
- Treat flood water as contaminated with sewage, chemicals and debris. Wash skin that touches it.
- Do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet or you are standing in water.
- Follow boil-water notices. See Making Water Safe under WATER.

Source: Ready.gov, Floods (public domain).

# Earthquake
tags: emergency, disaster, household, earthquake, drop cover hold on, aftershock, gas leak, tsunami

Most earthquake injuries come from falling objects and from people trying to move during the shaking. Drop where you are.

## Before

- Secure tall furniture, shelves and water heaters to the wall. Keep heavy objects low.
- Practise Drop, Cover and Hold On with everyone in the household.

## During

1. **Drop** to your hands and knees.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with your arms. Crawl under a sturdy table if one is close; otherwise get next to an interior wall, away from windows.
3. **Hold on** until the shaking stops.
4. In bed, stay there and cover your head with a pillow. Do not run outside: falling glass and masonry kill people at doorways.
5. Outdoors, stay outdoors, away from buildings, trees and power lines. In a car, pull over away from bridges and overpasses, stop and set the parking brake.

## After

- Expect aftershocks. Drop, Cover and Hold On again each time.
- Check yourself and others for injuries.
- If you smell gas, open windows, get everyone out, and do not use switches or flames. Shut off the gas only if you smell it.
- If trapped, cover your mouth with cloth, and tap on a pipe or wall or use a whistle instead of shouting, to save your strength and avoid breathing dust.
- On a coast, move inland or to high ground straight after strong shaking: a tsunami can follow within minutes.

Source: Ready.gov, Earthquakes (public domain).

# Wildfire and Smoke
tags: emergency, disaster, household, wildfire, fire, smoke, evacuation, air quality, mask

Wildfires move fast and can cut off roads. The safest choice is almost always to leave early. Smoke can harm people far from the fire.

## Before

- Clear dead plants, leaves and anything that burns from at least 1.5 m (5 ft) around the house.
- Plan two ways out of your neighbourhood. Keep the car fuelled and the go-bag ready.
- Pick a room you can keep clean of smoke: windows that close well, and a portable air cleaner if you have one.

## During

1. If told to evacuate, **leave immediately**. Do not wait to see the fire.
2. If you are trapped, call emergency services and give your location. Stay in a building or car rather than running through smoke.
3. Keep windows and doors closed. Run air conditioning on recirculate.
4. Outside in smoke, wear a well-fitting N95 or similar respirator. Cloth and paper masks do not stop smoke particles.

## After

- Return only when officials say it is safe. Watch for hot spots, ash pits and fallen lines.
- Wear a respirator and gloves while cleaning ash.

Source: Ready.gov, Wildfires (public domain).

# Hurricane and Severe Storm
tags: emergency, disaster, household, hurricane, storm, storm surge, tornado, wind, evacuation

Storm surge and inland flooding kill more people in hurricanes than wind does. Know your evacuation zone before a storm comes.

## Watch and warning

- A **watch** means the conditions are possible. Prepare now.
- A **warning** means they are expected. Finish preparing and follow evacuation orders.

## Before

- Learn your evacuation zone and route. Bring in or tie down outdoor furniture.
- Charge phones and power banks. Fill clean containers with water.

## During

1. If told to evacuate, **go**. Storm surge can flood areas far from the beach.
2. Sheltering at home: go to a small interior room without windows, on the lowest floor that will not flood.
3. Stay inside during the calm eye of the storm: the wind returns from the other direction.
4. Do not walk or drive through flood water.

## After

- Run generators only outdoors, far from windows, doors and vents.
- Stay away from fallen power lines and report them.

Source: Ready.gov, Hurricanes (public domain).

# Extreme Heat
tags: emergency, disaster, household, heat, heat wave, heat stroke, cooling, dehydration

Heat kills more people than most other weather. Older people, infants, people with chronic illness and outdoor workers are most at risk.

## During a heat wave

1. Stay in air conditioning if you can. Find the nearest cooling centre if you cannot.
2. When it is very hot, fans alone do not prevent heat illness. Take cool showers or baths.
3. Drink water regularly, before you feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol.
4. **Never** leave people or pets in a parked car, even for a few minutes.
5. Do heavy work in the coolest part of the day, and rest often in shade.
6. Check twice a day on older neighbours and people who live alone.

Know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke: see Heat Stroke under MEDICAL, First Aid Cards, and Cold and Heat Injuries.

Source: Ready.gov, Extreme Heat (public domain).

# Winter Storm
tags: emergency, disaster, household, winter, snow, ice, cold, hypothermia, frostbite, carbon monoxide

Winter storms bring cold, power cuts and dangerous roads. Many winter deaths come from car crashes, carbon monoxide and heart strain from shovelling snow.

## Before

- Keep a car kit with blankets, water, food, a shovel and sand or cat litter.
- Have a safe backup heat source and a carbon monoxide alarm with battery backup.

## During

1. Stay off the roads if you can. If you are stranded, stay with the car, run the engine about 10 minutes an hour for heat, and keep the exhaust pipe clear of snow.
2. Limit time outside. Wear layers, a hat and mittens. Watch for frostbite and hypothermia.
3. Use generators and grills only outdoors. Never heat the home with a gas oven or stove.
4. Keep pipes from freezing: let a tap drip, and keep the heating on.

Source: Ready.gov, Winter Weather (public domain).

# Shelter in Place
tags: emergency, disaster, household, shelter in place, chemical, radiation, nuclear, hazmat, seal room

When the air outside is dangerous, from a chemical release or radiation, the safest place is usually indoors. Follow the instructions officials give for your area.

## Chemical release

1. Get everyone and pets inside. Close and lock windows and doors.
2. Turn off fans, heating and air conditioning, and close the fireplace damper.
3. Go to an interior room above ground level with the fewest windows and vents.
4. Seal gaps around doors, windows and vents with plastic sheeting and duct tape.
5. Listen to the radio or phone alerts, and stay until told it is safe.

## Radiation

**Get inside. Stay inside. Stay tuned.**

1. Go to the basement or the middle of the building, away from outside walls and the roof.
2. If you were outside, take off your outer layer of clothing, bag it, and wash or wipe your skin and hair.
3. Plan to stay inside for at least 24 hours unless officials tell you otherwise.

Sources: Ready.gov, Chemical Emergencies and Radiation Emergencies (public domain).

# Evacuation
tags: emergency, disaster, household, evacuation, leave, routes, go bag, pets, plan

Leaving early, by a route you planned, is safer than leaving late in traffic. Use the meeting places and routes in the Plan module.

## Before

- Plan more than one route out, and where you will go: friends, family, a hotel or a public shelter.
- Keep the go-bag ready and the car fuelled. See Kits in the Stores module.
- Plan for pets: many public shelters take only service animals.

## When you leave

1. Follow official instructions and recommended routes. Do not take shortcuts that may be blocked.
2. Take the go-bag, medicines, documents, phone chargers and pets.
3. If there is time, unplug appliances, and turn off water, gas and electricity if told to.
4. Lock the house and tell your out-of-area contact where you are going.
5. Watch for washed-out roads and fallen power lines.

Source: Ready.gov, Evacuation (public domain).

# Pandemic and Home Isolation
tags: emergency, disaster, household, pandemic, infection, isolation, quarantine, hygiene, illness

In an outbreak, the goal is to stay healthy, avoid spreading illness, and care for sick people at home when hospitals are full.

## Before

- Keep at least a two-week supply of food, water, prescription and everyday medicines.
- Stay up to date on vaccinations.

## During

1. Wash hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, often.
2. Stay home when sick. Keep a sick person in a separate room, with their own towels and, if possible, bathroom.
3. Wear a well-fitting mask around sick people, and open windows to bring in fresh air.
4. Clean surfaces people touch often.
5. Keep a log of the sick person's temperature, fluids and symptoms in the Log module, to share with a clinician.

> **WARNING** Get medical help for trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, blue lips or face, or being unable to stay awake.

Source: Ready.gov, Pandemic (public domain).
